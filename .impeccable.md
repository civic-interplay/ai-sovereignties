## Design Context

### Users
Community members near a proposed site, journalists checking a fact from a
shared link, policy and council staff, and researchers. On a phone the main
job is **finding a site near me**: get to a place, tap a dot, read its record.
The national picture and the lenses come second on a phone; on desktop they
are the main surface.

### Brand Personality
Civic Interplay: plain, careful, public-interest. Three words: **plain,
sourced, open**. The interface should feel like a public record you can
check, not a product pitch. Plain language, no jargon, no editorial intros
(see Sarah's writing rules in ~/.claude/CLAUDE.md).

### Aesthetic Direction
- Light by default (reading and checking in daylight, beside other
  documents); dark map remains an option.
- Palette: Civic Interplay purple (#5b2a9e for labels, #7D50BD in charts),
  link blue #3f4aa8, story pink #b0436e, near-black ink #1a1d1b. Fira Sans.
- Data marks carry meaning; chrome stays quiet and recessive.
- Anti-references: glossy dashboards, neon-on-black, marketing hero layouts.

### Design Principles
1. The map is the content. Chrome gives way to it, most of all on a phone.
2. Every number and claim leads to its source in one tap.
3. Labels stand out by colour and weight; body text is dark and readable.
4. On a phone, adapt (bottom sheet, map first); never remove a function.
5. Never place a person at an address; stories are placed by town or district.
