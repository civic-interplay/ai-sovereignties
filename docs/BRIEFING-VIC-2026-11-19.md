# Victoria data centres: briefing to recite

*19 November 2026*

## 1. The numbers

### Victoria (tracker, corrected 18 Nov)

- **63 data centres:** 31 operating, 10 under construction, 8 approved, 7 awaiting a decision, 6 announced but not lodged, 1 refused.
- **What's operating now:** Melbourne is about **0.4–0.6 GW** (industry estimates: DC Byte about 0.4 GW, Fitch 580 MW across about 55 sites). Don't give the tracker's own operating total. Its MW figures are full-build campus numbers.
- **Pipeline:** the projects the tracker can size add up to **at least 4 GW**. Three-quarters of that is two proposals with no planning application: **Plumpton (2.4 GW)** and **Keppel Morwell (720 MW)**. Only about **0.9 GW** is approved or under construction.
- **Industry pipeline figure:** Melbourne **10.7 GW**, now ahead of Sydney's 7.8 GW (DC Byte, November).
- **Biggest single projects:** AirTrunk MEL2 354 MW, CDC Brooklyn 350 MW (whole campus at full build), STACK Truganina 252 MW.

### Australia

- **1.6 GW operating** across 163 data centres, forecast **3.9 GW by 2030** (Data Centres Australia / DC Byte).
- National pipeline about **26.8 GW**. Most of it won't be built: the report calls this "phantom demand".
- **The 100 MW in the Conversation piece** was one large data centre, not the national total. A correction is in.

## 2. How Victoria approves them

- **The fast-track (Development Facilitation Program):** in metro Melbourne, projects over A$20M go to the **Planning Minister, not the council**. 19 tracked sites went ministerial, 17 of them through this program.
- **Public notice:** **3 of 9** fast-track permits checked were publicly notified. This corrected the earlier claim that none were.
- **State data centre plan (22 Sep):**
  - no data centres in residential zones, and a **150 m buffer** from homes
  - recycled or non-drinking water for cooling, or offsets
  - operators **bring their own renewables** and pay grid connection costs
  - a **Local Investment Guarantee**, still in consultation
  - **no start date**, and **not retrospective**, so applications already lodged are exempt
- The figure of "eight applications before the Minister" comes from **ABC reporting**, not the government.

## 3. West Footscray

- **NEXTDC M3:** 150 MW, operating. The expansion (Stages 6 and 7, PA2604231) was lodged in February and notified in March. It's still undecided.
- **23 Sep:** Senate inquiry hearing in Yarraville, with a protest outside. Residents about **15 m** from the site asked for **buybacks**.
- **Late Sep:** Jemena put **66 kV power poles** in residential streets to supply M3. The council opposed them and the work paused.
- **21 Oct, NEXTDC to the ASX:** the pole work won't restart before **February 2027**, and the expansion decision is not expected **until 2027**.
- **The point:** grid connection deals are invisible, but the poles that deliver them aren't.

## 4. Councils, community benefit, the election

- **No signed community benefit agreement** exists for any Victorian data centre. The Local Investment Guarantee is the closest thing.
- **Councils lead the opposition:**
  - Hume was the first council to back an assessment framework (Aug 2025).
  - The MAV called for a **moratorium** on 30 Oct.
  - Outer Melbourne Councils: "Community benefit cannot be a vague promise."
  - Seven western councils want a pause.
- **Election, 28 Nov:**
  - **Labor:** the September rules, no moratorium.
  - **Coalition:** tighter rules and residential buffers, with no detail.
  - **Greens:** moratorium plus compensation.
  - **One Nation:** no data centres on farmland.
  - **Neither major party** has committed to giving councils back the decision, or to applying the new rules to projects already lodged.

## 5. Siting: "Do data centres need to be near people?"

- **Training** AI models can go wherever power is. **Inference and cloud services** cluster near cities.
- **Latency is a weak argument for chat.** An answer takes seconds to generate, and a round trip to the US adds about 0.15–0.2 s.
- **Stronger reasons they're near cities:**
  - data held onshore (sovereignty and residency rules)
  - **grid connections that already exist**
  - fibre and subsea cables
- **Near users ≠ next to homes.** West Footscray is a zoning failure.
- **Harm to neighbours:**
  - Documented harms are noise and diesel generator exhaust.
  - **No study yet measures health outcomes** for people living near data centres.
  - US setback rules run from about 60 m to 300 m.

## 6. Sydney comparison

- **Goodman Project Mars, Lane Cove West:** withdrawn in September. It was about 20 m from homes.
- **Kogarah Golf Club, Arncliffe:** the data centre was dropped on 8 Nov. The tracker marks it **Cancelled**, because the rest of the precinct application continues.
- **The pattern:** Sydney projects are retreating after opposition while Melbourne's pipeline overtakes Sydney's.

## Test Q&As

**Q: How many data centres does Victoria have?**
A: 31 operating, and another 31 in some stage from announced to under construction, on our tracker.

**Q: How much power do they use?**
A: Melbourne runs about half a gigawatt now. Proposals add at least 4 GW, but most of that isn't approved.

**Q: Isn't Victoria's pipeline 10 gigawatts?**
A: That's industry's total for everything proposed. Only about 0.9 GW on our tracker is approved or being built.

**Q: Who approves data centres in Victoria?**
A: For big metro projects, the Planning Minister through the fast-track, not the local council.

**Q: Do people get a say?**
A: Sometimes. Of the nine fast-track permits we checked, three were publicly notified.

**Q: Don't the new rules fix this?**
A: They have no start date, and they don't apply to applications already lodged, which includes the current wave.

**Q: What happened in West Footscray?**
A: Homes 15 m from a data centre, power poles in residential streets to feed its expansion, and a decision now pushed into 2027.

**Q: Do data centres have to be near where people live?**
A: Near cities, often yes, because of grid connections and data that must stay onshore. Next to homes, no. That's a zoning choice.

**Q: Isn't proximity about speed for AI?**
A: Less than you'd think. A chatbot takes seconds to answer, and the distance adds a fraction of a second.

**Q: Are data centres a health risk?**
A: Noise and diesel generators are real concerns, but no study has yet measured health outcomes for people living nearby.

**Q: Where do the parties stand?**
A: Labor and the Coalition both promise rules and buffers, and neither offers a moratorium. The Greens want a pause and compensation.

**Q: Are communities getting benefit agreements?**
A: Not yet. Victoria's Local Investment Guarantee is still in consultation, and councils say benefit "cannot be a vague promise".

**Trick question: "Victoria is ranked lowest for transparency, isn't it?"**
A: Don't affirm it unless you can name who ranked it. We couldn't find a source.
