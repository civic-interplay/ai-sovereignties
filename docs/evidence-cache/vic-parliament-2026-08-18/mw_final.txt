We have made our final decision on Melbourne Water’s prices for the
next 5 years
In April 2026, we released our draft decision on the price submission Melbourne Water submitted
to us in September 2025. 1 The draft decision set out our preliminary views on Melbourne Water’s
proposals, and invited interested parties to make further submissions. We also held a public forum
in April 2026. In addition to a response by Melbourne Water, we received seven written
submissions on our draft decision, which are available on our website (see Appendix A for details).
After considering feedback, we have made a price determination for Melbourne Water. 2 The price
determination sets out the maximum prices Melbourne Water may charge for prescribed services
(or the manner in which its prices are to be calculated, determined, or otherwise regulated) for the
5-year period from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2031. This final decision sets out our supporting reasons
and analysis for the price determination.
Where our final decision on a particular aspect is unchanged from our draft decision, we have
not detailed the supporting reasons in our final decision. Rather, we have noted that our final
decision confirms the reasons and position we reached in the draft decision.
Where we have reached a different decision to that proposed in our draft decision, or where
new information required our consideration, we have set out our reasons in full in this final
decision. This final decision should be read in conjunction with our draft decision.

Melbourne Water provides key services across Melbourne and surrounds
Melbourne Water provides wholesale water supply and sewerage services to retail water
businesses that we also regulate. Melbourne Water primarily provides bulk water and sewerage
services to the three metropolitan water businesses (Greater Western Water, South East Water,
Yarra Valley Water) and some bulk water services to four regional businesses, Barwon Water,
Gippsland Water, South Gippsland Water and Westernport Water.
Melbourne Water also provides waterways and drainage services in and around Melbourne.

1. Our role and approach to water pricing
1.1

We are Victoria’s independent economic regulator

Our role in the water industry is based on the Water Industry Regulatory Order 2014 (WIRO),
which is made under the Water Industry Act 1994 (WI Act) and sits within the broader context of
the Essential Services Commission Act 2001 (ESC Act). Our role under the WIRO includes
regulating the prices and monitoring service standards of the 18 water businesses operating in
Victoria.

1.2

We have reviewed Melbourne Water’s proposed prices

We have reviewed the prices Melbourne Water proposed to charge customers for prescribed
services from 1 July 2026. These services include:
• storage operator and bulk water services
• bulk sewerage services
• bulk recycled water services
• metropolitan waterways and drainage services. 9
Melbourne Water also provides wholesale water supply and sewerage services to the retail water
businesses that we also regulate. These retail water businesses are Greater Western Water, South
East Water, Yarra Valley Water, Barwon Water, Gippsland Water, South Gippsland Water and
Westernport Water. Melbourne Water also provides waterways and drainage services in and
around Melbourne.
Melbourne Water provided a submission to us proposing prices for a 5-year period starting 1 July
2026. Our task was to assess the price submission using the legal framework and make a price
determination that will take effect from 1 July 2026. We have made a price determination after
issuing a draft decision and considering feedback from interested parties, including Melbourne
Water’s response to the draft decision.
The price determination specifies the maximum prices that Melbourne Water may charge for
prescribed services, or the manner in which prices are to be calculated, determined or otherwise
regulated.
This final decision sets out our reasons for the price determination.

1.3

We assess prices against the WIRO and other legal requirements

Clause 11 of the WIRO specifies the mandatory factors we must have regard to when making a
price determination, including matters set out in the WIRO, the WI Act and the ESC Act. In making
a price determination, we have had regard to each of the matters required by clause 11 of the
WIRO, including:
• The objectives and matters specified in clause 8 of the WIRO, which include economic
efficiency and viability matters, industry specific matters, customer matters, health, safety,
environmental and social matters, and other matters which are specified in sections 8 and 8A of
the ESC Act and section 4C of the WI Act.
• The matters set out in section 33(3) of the ESC Act, which include the return on assets, relevant
benchmarking and any other matters that the commission considers relevant.
• The matters specified in our guidance. 10
• The principle that prices should be easily understood by customers and provide signals about
the efficient costs of providing services, while avoiding price shocks where possible.
• The principle that prices should take into account the interests of customers of the regulated
entity, including low income and vulnerable customers.
Appendix B lists the specific objectives and the various matters we must have regard to when
making a price determination and provides a guide to where we have done so in this final
decision. Table 1.1 summarises the matters we must have regard to and groups them into

1.4

PREMO

PREMO stands for Performance, Risk, Engagement, Management, and Outcomes (see Box 1.1).
Box 1.1

PREMO

Water businesses must demonstrate their level of ambition in delivering value for money for
customers in their price submissions across the five PREMO elements:
•

Performance
Have the performance outcomes to which the business committed in the previous
regulatory period been met or exceeded?

•

Risk
Has the business sought to allocate risk to the party best positioned to manage that
risk? To what extent has the business accepted risk on behalf of its customers?

•

Engagement
How effective was the business’s customer engagement to inform its price submission?

•

Management
Is there a strong focus on efficiency? Are controllable costs increasing, staying the
same, or decreasing? Is the price submission succinct and free of material errors?

•

Outcomes
Do proposed service outcomes represent an improvement, the status quo, or a
reduction of service standards?

The purpose of the PREMO framework is to provide incentives for water businesses to deliver
outcomes most valued by customers. PREMO includes reputation incentives via the rating of price
submissions as ‘Leading’ (the highest available rating), ‘Advanced’, ‘Standard’ or ‘Basic’. The
rating depends on the level of ambition expressed by a water business in its price submission and
its performance against commitments made in the previous price review. Financial incentives are
provided by linking the return on equity to the overall PREMO rating.
A key priority under PREMO is to provide incentives for a water business to engage with
customers to understand their priorities and concerns and to take these into account in forming its
proposals, as outlined in its price submission. These should be evidenced in price submissions by
linking the outcomes proposed with findings from a business’s engagement.

Our guidance specifies the way in which we expected Melbourne Water to assess its price

For Melbourne Water’s 2026 price review, its ambition in terms of delivering customer value was
assessed against all five elements of PREMO – Performance, Risk, Engagement, Management
and Outcomes. It is the first time the Performance element has been included for Melbourne
Water. This assesses the business’s performance against its outcomes and proposals from the
previous price review (which for Melbourne Water was in 2021). We did not assess the
Performance element in 2021. As it was the first time that we had applied the PREMO framework,
we did not have a set of previously approved outcomes to inform our assessment.
Taking into account all five elements of PREMO, a water business must self-assess and propose
an overall rating for its price submission as ‘Leading’, ‘Advanced’, ‘Standard’ or ‘Basic’. Its
proposed return on equity will then reflect its proposed PREMO rating. A ‘Leading’ submission has
the highest return on equity, and a ‘Basic’ submission the lowest. We assess the self-rating and
also assess the price submission more broadly, including the water business’s justification for the
proposed PREMO rating, and form our own view of the appropriate rating. This process
determines the PREMO rating adopted and the return on equity reflected in the revenue
requirement. 14

2.1

Final decision paper outline

This Melbourne Water Final Decision is structured around the steps we have taken to arrive at our

• Determine the regulatory period (section 2.2).
• Confirm the customer outcomes and service levels that Melbourne Water has committed to over
the regulatory period (chapter 3).
• Establish Melbourne Water’s revenue requirement using a building block methodology

• Use demand forecasts (chapter 5) and the form of price control (chapter 6) to convert the
revenue requirement to tariffs and prices (chapters 7 and 8).
Chapter 9 outlines our consideration of Melbourne Water’s proposed approach to estimating
developer charges, including its approach to large and/or complex connections.
Chapter 10 outlines our consideration of Melbourne Water’s financial position, which we have also
had regard to.
Chapter 11 outlines our assessment of Melbourne Water’s price submission under the PREMO
framework.

The revenue requirement is the forecast amount a water business needs to deliver on its customer

monitored by technical regulators including the Environment Protection Authority Victoria and the
Department of Health. 21 Along with forecast demand, it is an input to calculating prices.
We have used a building block methodology to establish the revenue requirement. This chapter
outlines our assessment of Melbourne Water’s revenue requirement based on the following steps:
• Establish an efficient benchmark level of forecast operating expenditure for the next regulatory
period (section 4.1).
• Establish an efficient benchmark level of forecast capital expenditure for the next regulatory
period (section 4.2).
• Roll forward the regulatory asset base (section 4.3).
• Apply a rate of return to the regulatory asset base, calculated using:
− a benchmark cost of debt estimated using a 10-year trailing average approach (section 4.4.1)
− a benchmark return on equity value determined by Melbourne Water’s PREMO rating
(section 4.4.2).
• Establish a return of capital through a regulatory depreciation allowance (section 4.5).
• Establish a benchmark tax allowance (section 4.6).

We disagree with Melbourne Water’s interpretation of the growth allowance
In seeking our reconsideration of the additional costs that we considered were growth related,
Melbourne Water stated that the expenditure growth allowance only relates to existing assets, not
new assets or customers who were not previously serviced by Melbourne Water. This
interpretation is incorrect. The expenditure growth allowance addresses all growth-related costs as
they apply to both existing and new operations. That is, it addresses increased costs associated
with higher throughputs on existing assets, and costs associated with expanding assets. It serves
as a simple proxy to escalate the cost allowance across all components of the operating
expenditure base as the customer base grows – labour, maintenance, operational costs (such as
water or wastewater treatment), and corporate overheads.
Given this, our final decision confirms our draft decision to not accept the additional costs proposed
by Melbourne Water that we consider are driven by growth and are therefore covered by the
2.0 per cent per year expenditure growth allowance (as shown in Table 4.5).
Inflation has been higher than some actual cost increases in recent years
Alongside this growth allowance, our pricing framework also allows for cost increases due to
inflation. Expenditure forecasts are set in real dollars, and in approving annual tariffs we apply an
inflation increment reflecting movements in the consumer price index for that year. Applying
inflation to the approved prices effectively assumes a corresponding inflationary increase to all
input costs. Like the expenditure growth rate, this applies equally across all components of the
operating expenditure base. Given individual input costs do not all rise directly in line with the
inflation rate, and some costs may even fall, this approach means some increases above inflation
may be effectively offset by increases below inflation.

6.1

Form of price control

Our final decision is to approve Melbourne Water’s proposed price cap form of price control for
its waterways and drainage charges, and a tariff basket form of price control for its water and
sewerage charges.
Melbourne Water proposed a price cap form of price control for its waterways and drainage
charges, and a tariff basket form of price control for its water and sewerage charges, with a
3 per cent rebalancing constraint.
Our draft decision accepted Melbourne Water’s proposed price cap form of price control for its
waterways and drainage charges. Among other things, we considered that a price cap provides
customers with price certainty and allows Melbourne Water to manage demand risk on behalf of its
customers, which we consider is more efficient than customers managing that risk. Further, it is a
continuation of Melbourne Water’s current approach and is consistent with our guidance
requirements.
Our draft decision accepted Melbourne Water’s proposed tariff basket form of price control for its
water and sewerage charges, subject to Ministerial approval of the South-Central Bulk Pool
Entitlements reform. Among other things, we considered that a tariff basket provides flexibility for
Melbourne Water to rebalance its water and sewerage tariffs during the regulatory period without

A principles based approach
We consider that a pricing principles approach, supported by clear and well-defined terms, strikes
an appropriate balance between transparency, certainty and flexibility. The approach provides
connection applicants with clarity regarding how charges will be determined and applied, thereby
improving predictability and supporting informed investment decisions. At the same time, it avoids
the need for highly prescriptive requirements that may become outdated or difficult to apply in a
rapidly evolving sector.
A key objective of this approach is consumer protection. By establishing clear pricing principles,
the framework promotes charges that are transparent, reasonable and aligned with the underlying
costs of providing services. This helps reduce the risk of inconsistent pricing outcomes,
inappropriate cost allocation, or the cross-subsidisation of connection applicants by existing
customers. The principles-based approach also enables businesses and us, as the regulator, to
respond to changing technologies, connection requirements and market conditions while
continuing to uphold these consumer protection outcomes.
Accordingly, we consider that a principles-based framework, underpinned by clear definitions and
guidance, best achieves our objective of providing transparency and certainty for connection
applicants while maintaining sufficient flexibility to remain effective in a fast-moving and evolving
sector. It also supports the efficient allocation of costs and helps ensure that the interests of
existing and future customers are appropriately protected.
The pricing principles set out in Melbourne Water’s price determination provide for the prices to be
determined with retailers (as a cost pass-through), or directly with connection applicants, where
this is permissible under the Water Act and other regulatory settings.

11. PREMO rating
We use the PREMO water pricing framework – Performance, Risk, Engagement, Management and
Outcomes – to review pricing proposals. PREMO is an incentive mechanism that links the return
on equity used to calculate a water business’s revenue requirement to that business’s level of
ambition expressed in its price submission and its delivery on commitments made as part of its last
price review. Our guidance required Melbourne Water to self-assess and rate its price submission
for each element of the PREMO mechanism and arrive at an overall self-rating. 88 We required
Melbourne Water to self-rate its price submission as either ‘Leading’, ‘Advanced’, ‘Standard’ or
‘Basic’, with ‘Leading’ being the most ambitious and ‘Basic’ the least.
The assessment tool included in our guidance directed Melbourne Water to consider its level of
ambition in relation to matters covered in its price submission, such as proposals related to
operating and capital expenditure, the form of price control, and tariffs.
We also assessed and rated Melbourne Water’s price submission. As outlined in our guidance, the
combination of Melbourne Water’s overall self-rating and our overall rating has determined the
return on equity we have adopted to calculate Melbourne Water’s revenue requirement in our final
decision.
For future price reviews, we intend to update our guidance in relation to First Nations rights and
interests, consistent with the principle of self-determination. We plan to include additional matters
that water businesses will need to demonstrate across the different elements of the PREMO
framework. This may impact how we assess whether a price submission has met our expectations
for achieving ‘Standard’, ‘Advanced’ or ‘Leading’ PREMO ratings in the future.
